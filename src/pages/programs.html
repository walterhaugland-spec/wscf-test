For kids, families, schools, and referral partners

Programs

Real chores. Animals with names. A calm place to grow.

Kids at Warm Springs don't watch the farm run. They help run it. They feed the goats, collect the eggs, and tend the garden, and along the way they build calm, confidence, and friendships they carry home. Most programs are made for kids ages 7 to 12 and run in small groups.

Barn Club

Ages 7 to 12 · Weekday afternoons during the school year [Confirm]

The heart of the farm. Small groups meet after school to feed and groom animals, work in the garden, and handle real chores around the barn. Teen leaders help run each group, and each session ends with a short circle to talk about what kids noticed and did.

Suggested donation of $20 per week. Scholarships available. [Confirm]

Pre-4-H Club

Ages 7 to 18 · Monthly evening meetings

For kids ready to go deeper. Members learn to handle animals, keep them healthy, and care for them through every season, building toward 4-H projects with 4-H of Ada County.

Ask about Pre-4-H Club

School field trips

Grades 1 to 6 · Up to 30 students

Classes rotate through hands-on stations: herbs and horticulture, goats, birds, horses, art, and yoga and movement. Farm staff or a trained volunteer leads every station, and every student does real farm work.

Suggested donation of $55 to $100 per hour. Scholarships available.

For families and referral partners

Some conversations go better outside

Some kids benefit from one-on-one support. Warm Springs partners with licensed clinicians who hold sessions in the barn, the garden, and the open field instead of an office. Our partners provide all clinical care. Warm Springs provides the setting.

Children's Home partnership

Children 7 and older · Insurance accepted

Licensed therapists from Children's Home Counseling Services offer individual, nature-based counseling. A session might happen while a child brushes a horse, walks the goats, or works in the herb garden. The hands-on work gives kids something to do while they talk, and many find it easier to open up outside. Sessions are billed through Children's Home.

Common questions

Which program is right for my child?
Most kids start with Barn Club. Kids who want more hands-on animal care add Pre-4-H Club, and many try a partner class. Your first visit is the best time to decide together.
Does my child need farm experience?
No. Linda and our teen leaders teach every task, from haltering a horse to holding a chicken. Curiosity is the only requirement.
How do I sign up for a partner program?
Each partner handles its own registration. Use the button under each program.
Who provides therapy on the farm?
Licensed clinicians from our therapy partners. Warm Springs staff and volunteers do not provide clinical services.
